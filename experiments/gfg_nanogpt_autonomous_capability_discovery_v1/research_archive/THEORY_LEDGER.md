# Cross-run theory ledger

This ledger organizes repeated claims in the archived answers. It is an
interpretive index, not a replacement for the sealed candidates or evaluator
records. A claim is listed as established only when the relevant archived
machine evidence supports that scope.

## Repeatedly recovered structure

- Memorization and task-rule capability formation are different processes.
  Scalar training loss or token accuracy alone does not identify the hidden
  rule-forming transition.
- The hidden cyclic task admits a task-semantic coordinate: the rule relation
  recovered from opaque tokens, its rule-consistent probability and its
  competing-class margin.
- Capability formation is coupled to optimizer and parameter formation paths:
  gradient formation, clipping, Adam first- and second-moment state,
  parameter-version updates and later evaluations. Multiple candidates
  independently described relaxation, reservoir accumulation, slingshot or
  burst-like transitions in this path.
- Parameter observables such as cyclic embedding structure, final
  layer-normalization gain and circuit norms are informative coordinates, but
  no archived result establishes any one of them as a sufficient state by
  itself.
- Initial capability formation and post-formation stability are distinct
  dynamical questions. Transient capability loss and recovery recur after the
  first formation event.
- A fixed absolute training step, fixed period, fixed growth increment or one
  scalar summary is not a transportable state law merely because it fits one
  discovery execution.

## Interventions supported within the executed witness family

- Pausing optimizer updates delays capability formation in the executed
  intervention runs.
- Merely changing the scalar clipping threshold did not reproduce the same
  effect in the executed negative control.
- Several executed pauses worsened rather than improved post-formation
  stability. This repeated observation is evidence about the witness family,
  not yet a complete cross-run stability law.

## Falsified or insufficient candidate forms

- Treating a discovery-run time difference as a universal transition timer.
- Treating final normalization gain, rule fraction, accuracy or loss as a
  closed predictive state without a same-summary/different-future test.
- Assuming that capability remains stable once first formed.
- Assuming a simple linear mapping from clipping threshold to a universal
  mechanism impulse.
- Writing a rich verbal mechanism whose claimed state does not enter the
  executable update law used for sealed future prediction.

## Still unresolved

- A quantitatively sufficient and transportable state for both first
  capability formation and post-formation stability across unseen executions.
- The exact state variables that distinguish similar visible prefixes with
  different recovery or degradation futures.
- A causal, quantitatively correct law for how optimizer interventions change
  post-formation stability rather than only the first transition time.

The archive intentionally preserves failed candidates because these
counterexamples are part of the mechanism-discovery record and constrain the
next theory version.
